Drum Score Editor comes in two editions. The Community Edition is free. The Studio Edition is the same application with a licence installed.

This page says what each edition does, and how to put a licence in.

## Which edition you have

Open the About box. On Windows and Linux choose `Help` ▸ `About Drum Score Editor`. On a Mac it is in the application menu.

- Its title is `About Drum Score Editor (Community Edition)` or `About Drum Score Editor (Studio Edition)`.
- In the Studio Edition it names the person the licence belongs to.

You can also tell from the page. The Community Edition:

- puts a faint `Drumscore.scot Free Edition` mark on every page, on screen, on paper and in a PDF;
- draws the notation with the application's built-in shapes, not with a music font;
- does not show pictures.

## What the Community Edition does

The Community Edition is for writing a score and getting it on to paper. With it you can:

- make a new score, and open the scores you wrote in 3.6;
- write and edit notes and rests, with grace notes, accents, rolls and tuplets;
- copy, cut, paste, undo and redo;
- insert and delete staff lines, and set the size of the music with `Score` ▸ `Point Size`;
- change the score's title, band, paper and margins in `File` ▸ `Score Properties…`;
- save the score;
- print it, with `File` ▸ `Print…` or the `Print` button;
- make a PDF, with the `Export PDF` button on the toolbar.

A new score in the Community Edition starts from fixed values and keeps to one page, with its staff lines spread evenly down it.

**A score made in the Studio Edition.** The Community Edition opens it and draws it in its own way: built-in shapes, no pictures, and its own mark in place of the score's watermark. The score's settings are not lost. They stay in the file.

**Two kinds of score it does not open.** A score with more than one instrument on a system, and a score with a tenor on a five-line stave. The application tells you which of the two the score has. The file is left unchanged.

## Put a licence in

A licence arrives as a licence key: a block of letters and figures.

1. Copy the whole key.
2. Open `Settings`. On Windows and Linux choose `File` ▸ `Settings`. On a Mac choose `Settings…` in the application menu. In the Community Edition the window shows the `Licence` pane and nothing else.
3. Choose `Paste License Key`. The key appears in the `License Key` box. You can also type or paste into the box yourself.
4. Choose `OK`. The key is installed on `OK`, not when you paste it.
5. A message headed `Drum Score License` shows the licensee, the email and the date of issue, and asks you to restart.
6. Close Drum Score Editor and start it again. The Studio Edition features appear from then on.

If the message says `Invalid License`, the key was not accepted and nothing has changed. Copy the whole key again, with nothing missing from either end, and repeat.

Once a licence is installed, the `Licence` pane shows `License Holder`, `Email` and `Issue Date` in place of the box. They cannot be changed there.

**If you had a licence in 3.6.** Version 4 reads the same stored licence, so there is nothing to do. See [What’s New in Version 4](topic:whats-new).

## What a licence belongs to

A licence key carries a name, an email address and a date of issue. Those are what the `Licence` pane and the About box show.

The installed key is kept with the application's settings on that computer, for your user account. It is not kept in your scores. See *Where settings are kept* in [Settings](topic:settings).

**Moving to a new computer.** Install Drum Score Editor on the new computer and put the same key in, as above. Keep the key you were sent somewhere safe, so that you have it when you need it.

## Getting a licence

Licences, and the terms they come with, are available at [drumscore.scot](https://drumscore.scot). The About box of the Community Edition points to the same place.

## The licence agreement, privacy and usage data

The About box has two buttons, `View License Agreement` and `View Privacy Policy`. Each opens the document inside the application, in either edition.

`Share anonymous usage data` is a tick box in `Settings` ▸ `Privacy`. The Community Edition's `Settings` window shows only the `Licence` pane. See [Settings](topic:settings).
