Drum Score Editor is a program for writing pipe band drum scores for snare, tenor and bass. It writes them the way they are written by hand, with the rudiments and marks of pipe band drumming. You type the notes, and it engraves them, spaces them and puts them on the page. It prints and makes PDFs, and the Studio Edition exports to other formats as well. It runs on Mac, Windows and Linux.

## Where to start

**New to Drum Score Editor.** Read [Starting a Score](topic:new-score), then [Entering Notes](topic:entering-notes), then [Rudiments, Rolls and Tuplets](topic:rudiments).

**Coming from version 3.6.** Read [What’s New in Version 4](topic:whats-new) first. Version 4 opens your 3.6 scores, but once it has saved one, 3.6 will not open that file. Read [Files and Older Scores](topic:files) before you change or save an old score.

**Looking something up.** Every key is in [Keyboard Shortcuts](topic:shortcuts). Every setting is in [Score Properties](topic:score-properties) or [Settings](topic:settings).

## The window

![The version 4 window with a score open: toolbars along the top, rudiments at the left, the note toolbox at the right](images/v4/main-window.png){: width="900" }

From the top:

- **The menu bar** is `File`, `Edit`, `View` and `Help`, with `Window` on a Mac. Most commands for a thing are not here. They are on the menu you get by right-clicking it. See [What’s New in Version 4](topic:whats-new).
- **The `Standard toolbar`** is about the file: new, open, save, print, PDF and `Properties`, then undo and redo, cut, copy and paste, and zoom. Show or hide it with `View` ▸ `Show` ▸ `Standard toolbar`. See [Files and Older Scores](topic:files) and [Printing and Export](topic:export).
- **The `Editing toolbar`** is about the music. Its buttons put marks on the notes you have selected: rolls, dots, ties, triplets, beams, unison, hairpins, accents and grace notes. Two more nudge the selection, and ten highlight pens are at the right-hand end. Show or hide it with `View` ▸ `Show` ▸ `Editing toolbar`. See [Rudiments, Rolls and Tuplets](topic:rudiments).
- **The score** is in the middle, in a tab that carries its name. Each score you open has a tab of its own. See [Files and Older Scores](topic:files).
- **The `Note toolbox`** is at the right. Its `Notes` tab writes notes, rests, barlines and time signatures, and its `Tenor` tab holds the tenor flourishes. Show or hide it with `View` ▸ `Show` ▸ `Note toolbox`. See [Entering Notes](topic:entering-notes).
- **The `Rudiments` panel** is at the left. It holds phrases you use often, ready to drag into a score. It is part of the Studio Edition. Show or hide it with `View` ▸ `Show` ▸ `Rudiments`. See [Rudiments, Rolls and Tuplets](topic:rudiments).
- **The `Quick Play` buttons** are above the note toolbox. They run a cursor through the music at the tunes' tempos, and record you playing along. They are part of the Studio Edition. Show or hide them with `View` ▸ `Show` ▸ `Quick Play`. See [What’s New in Version 4](topic:whats-new).
- **The `Status Bar`** is along the bottom. It says whether the score is saved, and has room for a short message, such as what Beautify left alone. Show or hide it with `View` ▸ `Show` ▸ `Status Bar`.

## How you work in it

There is a caret in the music, and a key or a toolbox button writes a note there: see [Entering Notes](topic:entering-notes). Marks go on the notes you have selected, from a key, the editing toolbar or the right-click menu: see [Rudiments, Rolls and Tuplets](topic:rudiments). In the Studio Edition, Beautify evens out the spacing, and a score runs on to as many pages as it needs by itself. A score made in the Community Edition keeps to one page, with its staff lines spread evenly down it. See [Layout and Spacing](topic:layout). Everything about one score's look is in `File` ▸ `Score Properties…`: see [Score Properties](topic:score-properties). In the Studio Edition, your own defaults for new scores are in [Settings](topic:settings).

## The two editions

The Community Edition is free. A licence makes it the Studio Edition: the same application, with a licence installed. Where this guide says something needs the Studio Edition, that is what it means. See [Licensing](topic:licensing). Licences, and the terms they come with, are at [drumscore.scot](https://drumscore.scot).

## Getting help

- **Questions and feedback** go to [alan@drumscore.scot](mailto:alan@drumscore.scot).
- **A licence or a lost key.** Write to [support@drumscore.scot](mailto:support@drumscore.scot). See [Licensing](topic:licensing).
- **If you are asked for a log**, choose `Help` ▸ `Show Log Folder`. It opens the folder that holds the log the application writes each time it starts.
- **For a newer version**, choose `Help` ▸ `Check for Updates…`.
- **The About box** shows your version and your edition. On Windows and Linux choose `Help` ▸ `About Drum Score Editor`. On a Mac it is in the application menu.
